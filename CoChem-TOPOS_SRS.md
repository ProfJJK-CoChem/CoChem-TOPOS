## Chapter 1: Introduction and Ecosystem Integration

### 1.1 CoChem-Base Ingestion and Frontend
CoChem-TOPOS does not operate in a vacuum; it is explicitly designed to be installed, configured, and driven by **CoChem-Base**. 
* **Ingestion Baskets**: CoChem-Base is responsible for the initial ingestion of structural data. It parses input geometries, smiles, or raw data into a preliminary "basket of isomers" which is then handed off to TOPOS for rigorous exploration and deduplication.
* **Unified GUI**: All user interaction, configuration, and Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) interventions (such as final basket sorting) are routed through the unified GUI hosted within CoChem-Base. CoChem-TOPOS serves as the backend topographical engine.

### 1.2 Data Movement and HDF5 Serialization
To ensure seamless transitions between modules (Base $\rightarrow$ TOPOS $\rightarrow$ TORQ), CoChem-TOPOS serializes all state into a standardized **HDF5 Database**.
* **`/deduplicated_isomers/` Schema**: Isomers, topological graphs, and quantum mechanical properties are saved into an `HDF5` landscape file. This prevents corruption, enables instant crash-recovery (via `MemmapIsomerBuffer.from_hdf5`), and allows large datasets to be efficiently moved across repositories without parsing massive JSON or text files.

## Chapter 2: The 11 Tiers of Calculation (Method Matrix)

The computational engine of CoChem-TOPOS is governed by an **11-Tier Method Matrix** that strictly dictates allowed combinations of quantum engines, methods, and basis sets to scale from fast screening to high-accuracy benchmarks.

* **Lower Tiers (Fast Screening)**: Utilizes semi-empirical methods like `GFN2-xTB` (via `xtb`) with no basis sets (`--basis none`) and Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials (MACE) for rapid coordinate scanning and initial geometry relaxations.
* **Middle Tiers (Production DFT)**: Utilizes `ORCA` with hybrid functionals (e.g., `B3LYP`, `wB97M-V`, `HF-3c`) paired with `def2-SVP` or `def2-TZVP` basis sets. Used for definitive geometry optimization and Hessian/frequency generation.
* **Upper Tiers (High Accuracy)**: Utilizes Coupled-Cluster methods (e.g., `CCSD(T)`) via `CFOUR` paired with correlated-consistent basis sets (`cc-pVXZ`) for exact thermodynamic and benchmark energetic comparisons.

## Chapter 3: Topological Search and Complex Triage

When a basket of isomers is received, CoChem-TOPOS initiates a rigorous topological analysis via `cochem_topos_graph` to classify the structural nature of the system before deploying quantum resources.

### 3.1 Weak Complex vs. Strong Complex Triage
The system mathematically evaluates shortest-distance atomic contacts to classify the input:
* **Weak Complex**: If the connections between distinct molecular fragments are purely non-covalent (e.g., hydrogen bonding, dispersion forces), the system tags it as a `Weak Complex`. The complex is mathematically severed (cleaved) into independent `MonomerSeeds`.
* **Strong Complex**: If the shortest distance connects to a transition metal coordination center (e.g., Fe, Pd, Ru, Pt), the cleavage is aborted. The system tags it as a `Strong Complex` and treats it as a single, unbreakable molecular entity.

### 3.2 Frozen Monomer Setup
To optimize the search space:
1. **Monomer First**: For Weak Complexes, the severed `MonomerSeeds` are first subjected to isolated isomer generation and deduplication running `ORCA` and `CREST`.
2. **Escalation**: Once the isolated monomers are fully characterized and deduplicated, the system escalates back to the complex level, freezing the optimized monomer geometries to explore the weak-interaction conformational space.

## Chapter 4: Isomer Generation and the "Jiggle Quench" System

For unified Strong Complexes or escalated Weak Complexes, TOPOS performs deep conformational exploration.

### 4.1 Multi-Engine Generation
Isomer generation is a multi-pronged search utilizing:
* **ORCA GOAT**: Global Optimizer-based conformational searches.
* **GFN2-xTB & CREST**: Metadynamics sampling to aggressively explore the potential energy surface.

### 4.2 Jiggle Quench Assembly
To ensure the search does not get trapped in local thermodynamic minima, TOPOS employs the **Jiggle Quench** system:
* **Jiggle (Perturbation)**: Geometries are systematically perturbed ("jiggled") by applying randomized physical force vectors to push the molecular system over higher energy barriers.
* **Quench (Relaxation)**: The perturbed geometries are immediately relaxed ("quenched") back to a local minimum using fast gradient-descent algorithms (often via xTB).
This approach ensures that high-barrier conformers, which standard thermal metadynamics might miss, are forcibly assembled and mapped into the HDF5 database.

## Chapter 5: Automated Isomer Deduplication

As the Jiggle Quench and multi-engine searches return thousands of candidate geometries, they are fed into the `TopologyCrusher` for aggressive deduplication.

### 5.1 Automated Pruning Metrics
* **Energetic Window**: Discards isomers outside a predefined kcal/mol window from the global minimum.
* **Heavy-Atom RMSD**: Uses quaternion Kabsch RMSD to merge geometrically identical structures.
* **Rotational Constants**: Merges isomers with identical rotational spectra constraints.

### 5.2 MolSym Symmetry-Group Filter
The system automatically detects molecular point group symmetry (using dynamic mendeleev atomic masses and the `molsym` package). The **Symmetry-Group Filter** instantly rejects pairs of molecules from being merged if they possess distinct point group symmetries (e.g., attempting to merge a $C_{2v}$ isomer with a $C_s$ isomer).

## Chapter 6: Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) and Basket Sorting

CoChem-TOPOS recognizes that automated deduplication cannot account for all chemical intuition. The final stage of the deduplication pipeline requires **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)** arbitration.

### 6.1 The HITL User Interface
* **Basket Sorting**: The surviving, deduplicated isomers are organized into graphical "baskets" presented to the user via the unified CoChem-Base GUI.
* **Final Decisions**: The user can manually inspect the baskets, sort out false positives, or force the merging of specific conformer states that the automated RMSD filters kept separate.

### 6.2 Manual Symmetry Assignment
While the MolSym filter operates automatically, the GUI explicitly provides the user the ability to manually assign or override the symmetries of specific parts of the complex. This ensures that fluxional molecules or specific functional group rotations are handled exactly as the chemist intends before the final ensemble is sealed.

## Chapter 7: Handoff to CoChem-TORQ

Once the human user validates the final basket of isomers and commits the changes, the `TopologyCrusher` seals the final ensemble into the HDF5 `/deduplicated_isomers/` dataset. 

This finalized, rigorously explored, and deduplicated HDF5 database is the exact artifact consumed by **CoChem-TORQ**. CoChem-TORQ takes these locked structural states and subjects them to transition state (TS) searches, reaction path (NEB) mapping, and kinetic rate calculations, entirely bypassing the need to re-explore the conformational space.
